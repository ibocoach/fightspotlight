Natália Silva Wins Vacant Women's Flyweight Title Over Wang Cong

Natália Silva used her speed to build an early lead and held off a late surge from Wang Cong to become the new UFC women's flyweight champion in Salt Lake City.

UFC·3 Oct 2026·UFC 332·By Fight Spotlight

The UFC has a new women's flyweight champion. Headlining UFC 332 at the Delta Center in Salt Lake City on 3 October 2026, Natália Silva defeated Wang Cong by unanimous decision — 48-47, 48-47 and 49-46 — to claim the vacant title.

How the Title Became Vacant

The championship was open after Valentina Shevchenko was forced to vacate the belt due to injury. That opened the door for Silva, one of the division's most consistent contenders, and Wang, who stepped into the biggest fight of her career. With the win, Silva became the fifth champion in the history of the women's flyweight division, according to the UFC.

Speed Sets the Tone

From the first exchanges, the difference in hand and foot speed was clear. Wang appeared to struggle with Silva's quickness early, and the Brazilian was able to land and move before her opponent could respond. Wang briefly found herself on the ground in the opening round, although she quickly returned to her feet.

The key moment came midway through the second round, when Silva dropped Wang with a sharp head kick. It was the clearest scoring blow of the fight and helped Silva build a lead that Wang would spend the rest of the night chasing.

Wang's Late Push

To her credit, Wang never stopped coming forward. She found success with solid body kicks in the middle rounds and pushed hard for a finish in the fifth, producing some of her best work of the night. But Silva landed enough in return to stay in control, and Wang's surge came too late to overturn the deficit on the scorecards.

The main event capped a record-setting night in Salt Lake City. The card produced 11 knockouts, which theScore and other outlets reported as the most in a single UFC event.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

Silva's victory was built on the qualities that carried her to a title shot: speed, timing and composure. She did not need to take unnecessary risks. Instead, she established her range, scored the defining moment of the fight with the second-round head kick, and managed the remaining rounds intelligently.

The closing stages, though, offered lessons for both fighters. Wang's strongest work came in the final round, which raises an obvious question about what might have happened had she increased her output earlier. For Silva, those late exchanges are a reminder that the champion's challengers will not fade quietly.

For Wang, this was a breakthrough performance despite the defeat. Taking a five-round title fight against an elite contender and competing to the final bell shows she belongs among the division's best, and a rematch could become a natural option in future.

The fight also highlighted the value of early scoring in a five-round title contest. Silva's second-round knockdown carried weight on the judges' cards long after it happened, and it gave her the freedom to fight more conservatively in the later rounds while Wang was forced to take risks.

For Silva, the belt is the reward for years of steady progress. Her first title defence will reveal whether she can build the kind of reign the division saw under Shevchenko, but on this evidence she has the tools to do it.
