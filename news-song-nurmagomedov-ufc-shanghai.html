Song Yadong Knocks Out Umar Nurmagomedov in Shanghai

Fighting in front of a home crowd, Song Yadong caught Umar Nurmagomedov with a right uppercut as he shot for a takedown, producing a major upset in the UFC Shanghai main event.

UFC·29 Aug 2026·UFC Fight Night: Nurmagomedov vs. Song·By Fight Spotlight

Song Yadong produced one of the biggest upsets of the year in front of his home crowd. In the main event of UFC Fight Night: Nurmagomedov vs. Song in Shanghai on 29 August 2026, Song knocked out Umar Nurmagomedov with a right uppercut at 1:48 of the second round.

A Homecoming Against a Heavy Favourite

Nurmagomedov entered the non-title bantamweight bout as a heavy favourite, regarded by many as a future champion. He had only one previous professional loss, and he had never been finished. Song, meanwhile, was fighting at the Oriental Sports Center in his home country, carrying the expectations of a crowd that had waited for this moment.

Round One: Nurmagomedov's Wrestling

The opening round followed the pattern many expected. Nurmagomedov caught a Song low kick and converted it into a double-leg takedown, the only takedown of the round. Song refused to be controlled, however, threatening a guillotine choke from underneath before sweeping his way back to his feet and seeing out the round.

Round Two: The Upset

In the second round, Song stuffed a Nurmagomedov takedown attempt with a well-timed sprawl. A clash of heads opened a large cut over Song's right eye, but it would not matter. When Nurmagomedov shot in again, Song met him with a right uppercut that dropped him and ended the fight at 1:48 of the round.

It was the first time Nurmagomedov had been stopped in his professional career, and only the second loss of his record.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

The finish was a textbook example of timing over power. Nurmagomedov's game is built on getting opponents to the mat, and once Song had seen the entry in the first round and stopped it in the second, he was ready for it. The uppercut is the ideal counter to a level change, catching an opponent as his head drops forward, and Song placed it perfectly.

Song's first-round defence deserves credit too. Being taken down by a fighter with Nurmagomedov's grappling pedigree is often the beginning of a long night, but Song's guillotine threat and sweep prevented any sustained control and gave him confidence going into the second.

The result also continued a strong run for Song. According to the UFC, it was his second consecutive knockout of a former title challenger, and he followed the win by calling for a shot at the bantamweight championship. Given the profile of the opponent and the setting, his argument has gained real weight.

The setting made the moment even bigger. Headlining an event in his home country, with the weight of local expectation behind him, Song had every reason to fight tentatively against a favourite of Nurmagomedov's calibre. Instead he stayed patient, survived the early grappling and delivered a finish that will be replayed for years. It was the kind of performance that can redefine a fighter's career.

For Nurmagomedov, the setback is significant but not defining. He controlled the first round and remains one of the most skilled grapplers at 135 pounds. The challenge now is to show he can adjust when opponents are ready for his takedowns.

For the bantamweight division, the result adds another strong name to the list of contenders, and gives Shanghai a night its fans will not forget.
