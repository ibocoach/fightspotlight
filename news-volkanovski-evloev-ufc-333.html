Volkanovski Ends Evloev's Unbeaten Record With Round 2 TKO

Alexander Volkanovski weathered Movsar Evloev's early wrestling and stopped the previously unbeaten challenger with six seconds left in the second round to keep the featherweight title.

UFC·24 Oct 2026·UFC 333·By Fight Spotlight

Alexander Volkanovski answered every question about his age and his place in the featherweight division in Abu Dhabi. Headlining UFC 333 at Etihad Arena on 24 October 2026, the 38-year-old champion stopped Movsar Evloev by TKO at 4:54 of the second round, handing the undefeated challenger the first loss of his professional career.

Evloev's Wrestling Makes Its Mark

Evloev entered the bout unbeaten and with a reputation as one of the division's most effective wrestlers, and he showed why in the opening round. He went to work quickly with his trademark takedowns and had real success, forcing Volkanovski to work defensively and spend energy getting back to his feet. Live scorecards from ringside were split on who had won the first five minutes.

Into the second round, the contest remained finely balanced. Evloev was holding his own with the champion through the middle of the round, and there was little to suggest the fight was about to end.

One Right Hand Changes Everything

The turning point came late in the second. Volkanovski landed a clean right hand that prompted Evloev to turn away as he backed out of range — a rare moment of vulnerability from a fighter who had rarely been hurt in his career. Volkanovski recognised it immediately, tracked him down and swarmed with strikes, putting Evloev on the canvas and finishing him on the ground. ESPN's results listed the stoppage as coming from punches.

The referee waved it off with just six seconds remaining in the round. Volkanovski was later awarded a Performance of the Night bonus for the finish.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

This was a classic championship performance built on composure. Volkanovski did not panic when Evloev's wrestling troubled him early, and he did not force his offence. Instead, he waited for the single opening that a fighter of his experience knows will eventually come, and when it arrived he punished it without hesitation.

The result also reframes the conversation around Volkanovski's career. At 38, he is competing in a division traditionally dominated by younger athletes, and he has now beaten an undefeated contender who many believed was the stylistic nightmare to finally end his reign. The finish, rather than a points win, makes the statement louder still.

For Evloev, the defeat is a significant setback but not a verdict on his ability. He won the opening round on some cards and showed his grappling can trouble the very best at 145 pounds. A fighter who reached a title shot without a loss will have a route back to contention.

There is also a tactical lesson in how the finish arrived. Evloev's success came when he could initiate grappling exchanges on his own terms; the moment he was forced to react to Volkanovski's striking, his defence broke down. Future challengers will note that the champion's danger only grows the longer a fight stays standing.

Attention now turns to who is next for the champion. Several contenders were quick to make their interest known after the event, and with Volkanovski showing no signs of decline, the featherweight division's top tier will be eager for their opportunity.

UFC 333's co-main event also delivered a title fight, as Petr Yan defended the bantamweight championship against Merab Dvalishvili in the third chapter of their rivalry.
