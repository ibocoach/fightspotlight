Gane Outpoints Hokit to Retain Heavyweight Title at UFC 334

Ciryl Gane survived an early onslaught from Josh Hokit and rallied late to keep the undisputed heavyweight championship by unanimous decision at Madison Square Garden.

UFC·14 Nov 2026·UFC 334·By Fight Spotlight

Ciryl Gane is still the UFC heavyweight champion. In the main event of UFC 334 at Madison Square Garden on 14 November 2026, the Frenchman defeated Josh Hokit by unanimous decision, with all three judges scoring the contest 48-47. It was the narrowest possible margin on every card, and it capped 25 minutes that tested Gane far more than many expected.

A Fast, Physical Start From Hokit

Hokit arrived at Madison Square Garden challenging for a championship in just his fifth UFC appearance, and he fought like a man with nothing to lose. He changed levels almost immediately and took Gane down in the opening round, signalling that he had no intention of standing at range with one of the most technical strikers the division has seen.

Gane's jab remained his most reliable weapon throughout, repeatedly snapping Hokit's head back and keeping the challenger from settling into a rhythm. But Hokit's relentlessness and awkward timing kept him in exchanges, and his heavy right hand became an increasingly common sight as the fight wore on.

The Middle Rounds Swing Toward the Challenger

The middle stretch of the fight belonged largely to Hokit. Ringside reports described a powerful slam takedown in the closing minute of one round and an obvious drop in Gane's energy as the pace refused to slow. Between rounds, Gane's corner urged him to use his time on top to recover — an unusual instruction for a champion whose game is built on movement and volume.

For a period, the upset looked genuinely possible. Hokit was pressing forward, landing his right hand more often, and forcing Gane to spend energy defending wrestling entries rather than building his own offence.

Gane Finds His Second Wind

The championship rounds told a different story. Gane looked like the fresher fighter in the fifth, defending Hokit's early shot and answering with knees and elbows at close range. With the fight decided by a single point on each scorecard, his ability to win the late exchanges was ultimately the difference between keeping and losing the belt.

Not everyone agreed with the result. MMA Mania described the verdict as controversial, a reflection of how evenly the middle rounds were contested. Officially, however, the verdict was unanimous.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

This was a performance about resilience rather than dominance. Gane has long been regarded as a stylist, but against Hokit he had to show he could absorb pressure, lose rounds and still find a way to win. That is a valuable quality in a heavyweight champion, even if it was not the clean showcase he would have wanted on a Madison Square Garden card.

For Hokit, the result is a defeat that may enhance his standing. Few fighters reach a UFC title fight in five appearances, and fewer still push the champion to the final round with the belt in the balance. His wrestling, cardio and willingness to trade made the heavyweight picture more interesting overnight.

The bigger question is what comes next for Gane. A champion who has just been pushed this hard will face renewed scrutiny, and an immediate rematch is likely to be part of the conversation after a contest this close. What is certain is that the heavyweight division now has a clear, credible challenger in Hokit, and a champion who has proved he can win ugly when he needs to.

UFC 334 also featured a second title fight, as Kayla Harrison defended the women's bantamweight championship against Amanda Nunes in the co-main event.
