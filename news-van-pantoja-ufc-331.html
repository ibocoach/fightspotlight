Joshua Van Retains Flyweight Title in Pantoja Rematch

Joshua Van turned back Alexandre Pantoja's grappling and dropped the former champion in the fifth round to win their rematch by unanimous decision in Los Angeles.

UFC·19 Sep 2026·UFC 331·By Fight Spotlight

Joshua Van removed any doubt about his status as flyweight champion. Headlining UFC 331 at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles on 19 September 2026, Van defeated Alexandre Pantoja by unanimous decision, with official scores of 49-46, 48-47 and 50-45, to retain the title.

Why the Rematch Mattered

Van first took the belt from Pantoja at UFC 323 in December 2025, but that fight ended prematurely when Pantoja was injured. The outcome left questions unanswered for both men: Van had the title, but not the clean victory that would confirm him as the division's best. UFC 331 was billed as the chance to settle it.

Pantoja Makes It a Fight

The former champion was far from outclassed. Pantoja held his own on his feet and had his best moments in the first and fourth rounds, when he was able to get hold of Van and drag the fight to the mat. ESPN reported that he accumulated close to ten minutes of control time across the five rounds.

But the defining feature of the night was Van's takedown defence. Outlets differed on the exact figures, but all agreed he stopped the clear majority of Pantoja's attempts and prevented the Brazilian from holding him down for long stretches. Each failed entry gave the younger champion another opportunity to strike.

A Fifth-Round Statement

Van saved his best for last. Midway through the fifth round, he dropped Pantoja with a right hand — the only knockdown of the fight — and followed up with heavy strikes on the ground and a kimura attempt. When Pantoja shot for another takedown shortly after, Van ended up back on top. It was the clearest round of the contest and a fitting finish to a fight that Yahoo Sports described as a Fight of the Year thriller.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

This was the performance Van needed. Beating Pantoja after an injury stoppage would always come with an asterisk; beating him over five full rounds, while neutralising the grappling that made him one of the best flyweights in UFC history, removes it entirely.

The champion's composure stood out. Pantoja's style is designed to drown opponents in scrambles and wear them out, yet Van seemed stronger as the fight went on. His fifth-round knockdown came at the point when many fighters would be content to protect a lead.

Tactically, the rematch reinforced how Van's defensive wrestling unlocks his offence. Every stuffed takedown became an opportunity to strike as Pantoja disengaged, and over 25 minutes those small moments added up to a clear edge on the scorecards. It is a blueprint other contenders will find difficult to counter.

For Pantoja, there is no shame in this defeat. He competed hard for 25 minutes and won rounds against a champion in the prime of his career. Where he goes next will be one of the more intriguing questions at 125 pounds.

For Van, still only 24, the challenge now is building a reign. With his status confirmed, the flyweight division's contenders know exactly who they need to beat — and how difficult that will be.

In the co-main event, Arman Tsarukyan knocked out Mauricio Ruffy with elbows at the end of the first round.
