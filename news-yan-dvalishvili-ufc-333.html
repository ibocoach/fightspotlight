Yan Edges Dvalishvili in Trilogy to Retain Bantamweight Title

After losing the first two rounds to Merab Dvalishvili's wrestling, Petr Yan shut down the takedowns and took over to win their trilogy by unanimous decision.

UFC·24 Oct 2026·UFC 333·By Fight Spotlight

The trilogy is settled, and Petr Yan holds the advantage. In the co-main event of UFC 333 at Etihad Arena in Abu Dhabi on 24 October 2026, Yan defeated Merab Dvalishvili by unanimous decision, with all three judges scoring it 48-47. The win was Yan's first successful defence of the bantamweight championship and gives him a 2-1 lead in one of the division's defining rivalries.

The Road to a Third Fight

The two men first met in March 2023, when Dvalishvili won a lopsided unanimous decision, scored 50-45 on all three cards. Yan reversed that result at UFC 323 in December 2025, taking the title in the rematch. That set up a deciding third fight, with the belt once again on the line.

Dvalishvili Controls the Opening Rounds

For ten minutes, the trilogy looked as though it might follow the script of their first encounter. Dvalishvili's relentless wrestling pace dominated the opening two rounds, and he scored five hard-earned takedowns across that stretch. The official scorecards confirm that all three judges gave him both of the first two rounds.

Yan Takes Over

The fight changed in the third round. Yan began to defend every takedown attempt Dvalishvili threw at him, and with the wrestling neutralised, he was free to strike. Body kicks and knees became his primary weapons as he started to dictate where the fight took place.

The numbers illustrate the scale of the shift. According to post-fight statistics reported by Bleacher Report and UFCalendar, Dvalishvili landed five takedowns from 58 attempts over the full fight, all of them in the first two rounds. The judges awarded Yan rounds three, four and five, producing identical 48-47 scorecards. The bout also earned a Fight of the Night bonus.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

This was a masterclass in mid-fight adjustment. Being down two rounds to Dvalishvili is a position few fighters recover from, because his pace usually accelerates as opponents tire. Yan did the opposite of what was expected: he got stronger, solved the takedown problem and then made Dvalishvili pay for every failed attempt.

His takedown defence in the final 15 minutes was the decisive factor. Dvalishvili kept shooting, but each stuffed attempt cost him energy and gave Yan another chance to land on the counter. By the championship rounds, the dynamic of the rivalry had flipped completely.

The scorecards also tell a story about how the rivalry has evolved. In their first meeting Dvalishvili swept every round; in the third, he won only the first two. Yan has steadily closed the gap with each fight, and in Abu Dhabi he finally turned the wrestling battle in his favour.

For Dvalishvili, the loss is narrow and hard to take. He won the first two rounds clearly and remains one of the best bantamweights in the world, but the result means he trails in the series and faces a longer path back to the title.

For Yan, the win carries extra weight. A first title defence is often the hardest, and achieving it against the man who once beat him so decisively confirms his place at the top of the division.

In the night's main event, Alexander Volkanovski retained the featherweight title with a second-round TKO of Movsar Evloev.
