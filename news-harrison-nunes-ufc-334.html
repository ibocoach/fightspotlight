Harrison Submits Nunes in Round 4 to Retain Bantamweight Title

Kayla Harrison spoiled Amanda Nunes' long-awaited return, retaining the women's bantamweight championship with a fourth-round rear-naked choke at Madison Square Garden.

UFC·14 Nov 2026·UFC 334·By Fight Spotlight

The most anticipated women's fight of the year ended with Kayla Harrison's hand raised. In the co-main event of UFC 334 at Madison Square Garden on 14 November 2026, Harrison submitted Amanda Nunes with a rear-naked choke at 1:29 of the fourth round to retain the UFC women's bantamweight championship.

A Long-Awaited Showdown

The fight had been building since Harrison won the title at UFC 316 in June 2025, submitting Julianna Peña with a kimura. Nunes, who walked away from mixed martial arts roughly three and a half years earlier while still the reigning bantamweight champion, entered the Octagon after that victory to set up the matchup. Her return turned the bout into one of the biggest attractions on the UFC calendar.

Nunes Starts Fast

Nunes showed little sign of rust in the early going. She came forward aggressively and had real success on the feet, out-landing Harrison in stretches of the striking exchanges. According to post-fight reports, she also became the first fighter to take Harrison down in the UFC, doing so in the second round — a notable achievement against an Olympic judo champion.

But the fight's broader pattern favoured the champion. Harrison's grappling was the deciding factor in every round, and according to the UFC's own recap she finished each of the first three rounds in top position, steadily accumulating control time and forcing Nunes to work from underneath.

The Finish

The end came early in the fourth. Harrison caught a Nunes kick, used it to put the former champion on the canvas and quickly took her back. From there she locked in the rear-naked choke, and Nunes was forced to tap at 1:29 of the round.

It was a finish that summed up the contest: Nunes had moments of danger on the feet, but each time the fight hit the mat, Harrison was in charge.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

This victory changes how Harrison's career will be remembered. Beating Nunes — widely regarded as one of the greatest female fighters in history — gives her a signature win that no other result could match. Several outlets framed it as a legacy-defining night, and some went further, placing her in the conversation for the greatest of all time.

Tactically, the champion's approach was disciplined. She accepted that she would lose some striking exchanges, refused to be drawn into a firefight, and trusted that her clinch work and top control would wear Nunes down over five rounds. By the fourth, that investment paid off.

For Nunes, the defeat does not erase an extraordinary career, and her performance in the opening rounds showed she remains dangerous at the highest level. Whether she continues competing is an open question.

The same uncertainty surrounds the champion. Afterwards, Harrison was noncommittal about her own future, leaving the next chapter of the women's bantamweight division unclear. Whatever she decides, UFC 334 delivered the result that will define this era of the division.

The night's main event saw Ciryl Gane retain the heavyweight championship with a unanimous decision over Josh Hokit.
