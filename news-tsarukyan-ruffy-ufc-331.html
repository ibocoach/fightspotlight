Tsarukyan Knocks Out Ruffy With Elbows at UFC 331

With seconds left in the opening round, Arman Tsarukyan found a perfectly timed elbow from the clinch to knock out Mauricio Ruffy in the UFC 331 co-main event.

UFC·19 Sep 2026·UFC 331·By Fight Spotlight

Arman Tsarukyan delivered one of the most dramatic finishes of the year in the UFC 331 co-main event. At Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles on 19 September 2026, Tsarukyan knocked out Mauricio Ruffy with elbows at 4:56 of the first round in a non-title lightweight bout.

Ruffy Stops the Wrestling

Tsarukyan is known as one of the lightweight division's most effective wrestlers, and he went looking for takedowns throughout the round. Ruffy, a striker with a reputation for knockouts, had clearly prepared for it. Reports agreed that he stuffed almost all of Tsarukyan's attempts; one count had Tsarukyan landing just one of ten.

With the wrestling largely neutralised, the round looked as though it might become a test of whether Tsarukyan could deal with Ruffy's power on the feet over the course of a full fight.

The Finish

The answer came in the final seconds. Working from the clinch, Tsarukyan landed a left elbow to Ruffy's face, followed by a right elbow as Ruffy fell to the canvas. The UFC's own account described a pair of quick, hard elbows that landed clean and put Ruffy out. The stoppage was confirmed at 4:56 — four seconds before the end of the round — and Tsarukyan was awarded a Performance of the Night bonus.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

The finish showed how dangerous Tsarukyan is when his first plan does not work. Ruffy won the wrestling battle almost completely, which is exactly what he needed to do. But in defending those takedowns, he spent long stretches tied up with Tsarukyan in the clinch — and that is where the knockout came from.

It is a lesson in how threats combine. Ruffy had to commit to stopping the takedown, and that commitment left him exposed to short strikes at close range. Tsarukyan did not need to win the wrestling exchanges outright; he only needed the clinch positions they created.

The victory extended Tsarukyan's winning streak to six, according to ESPN, and he used his post-fight moment to call for a title shot. Given the quality of the opponent and the manner of the win, his case is difficult to dismiss. Ruffy entered the bout regarded as one of the division's most dangerous strikers, and beating him by knockout — rather than grinding out a decision — sends a clear message.

The timing of the finish added to its impact. Ending a fight with seconds left in a round requires both awareness and composure, and Tsarukyan showed neither urgency nor hesitation. Rather than chasing a takedown to steal the round, he capitalised on the position in front of him.

For Ruffy, the defeat is a painful way to lose a fight he was arguably executing well. His takedown defence was outstanding, and the loss came from one moment rather than a sustained pattern. He remains a dangerous presence in the lightweight top tier.

The lightweight title picture will now have to account for Tsarukyan. Whether the UFC grants him the opportunity next remains to be seen, but few contenders have produced a more persuasive argument in a single round.

In the UFC 331 main event, Joshua Van retained the flyweight title with a unanimous decision over Alexandre Pantoja.
