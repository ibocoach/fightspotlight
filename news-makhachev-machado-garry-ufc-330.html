Makhachev Retains Welterweight Title, Sets UFC Record With 17th Straight Win

Islam Makhachev survived a determined challenge from Ian Machado Garry to retain the welterweight title in Philadelphia and claim the longest winning streak in UFC history.

UFC·15 Aug 2026·UFC 330·By Fight Spotlight

Islam Makhachev has rewritten the UFC record books. Headlining UFC 330 at Xfinity Mobile Arena in Philadelphia on 15 August 2026, Makhachev defeated Ian Machado Garry by unanimous decision — 49-46, 49-46 and 48-47 — to retain the welterweight championship and secure his 17th consecutive UFC victory.

That streak sets a new benchmark for consecutive wins in the promotion. As reported by Al Jazeera and TNT Sports, it surpasses the record previously held by Anderson Silva.

Round Two: The Knockdown

Machado Garry was competitive and active in the clinch early, but the second round produced the fight's first major moment. As the two separated, Makhachev landed a right hand and a left high kick that put the challenger down. He immediately followed with a takedown and went to work with the wrestling that has defined his career.

Many fighters would not have recovered. Machado Garry did — fending off further takedown and submission attempts and fighting his way back into the contest.

Round Three: Machado Garry's Best Round

The third round belonged to the challenger. Machado Garry found a rhythm with body kicks, head kicks and a sharp jab, and reports noted that he bloodied Makhachev's nose. For a stretch, it was the champion who was being forced to adjust.

Round Five: Sealing It

With the fight still competitive heading into the final round, Makhachev returned to his strongest weapon. His first attempt only briefly put Machado Garry on the mat, but the second was complete: he took the challenger's back immediately and locked in a body triangle. Machado Garry survived to the final bell, but the sequence cemented the decision.

Fight Spotlight Analysis

The scorecards suggest a comfortable night, but the fight was closer than the numbers indicate. Machado Garry pushed Makhachev in a way few opponents have during his streak, and the third round in particular showed that the champion can be troubled by a long, disciplined striker.

What separated them was Makhachev's ability to produce decisive moments. The second-round knockdown and the fifth-round back take were the two clearest passages of the fight, and both came from the champion.

The record itself is the headline. Seventeen straight wins at the highest level, across multiple weight classes, reflects not just skill but remarkable consistency. Makhachev has become the measure against which the rest of the welterweight division will be judged.

Tactically, the champion's approach followed a familiar blueprint. Makhachev does not need to win every exchange on the feet; he needs to create moments where his opponent's attention shifts, then turn them into takedowns and control. The head kick in the second round and the back take in the fifth both followed that pattern, and both came at moments when Machado Garry appeared to be finding his rhythm.

The challenge for the welterweight division is clear. Contenders now know that even a strong performance over several rounds may not be enough against a champion who can change the course of a fight in a single sequence.

Machado Garry, who falls to 17-2, leaves with his reputation enhanced. He survived adversity in the second, won rounds against the best fighter in the world, and proved he belongs in championship company.

In the co-main event, Mackenzie Dern retained the women's strawweight title with a unanimous decision over Gillian Robertson.
